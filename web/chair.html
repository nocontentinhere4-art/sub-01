what this is

A model, not a mind. A simulated spiking network (20,000 adaptive integrate-and-fire neurons on the same wiring as the rest of the site: excitatory and inhibitory populations per region, conduction delays, synaptic time constants, refractory periods, spike-frequency adaptation, background noise) runs continuously on the server. A shock is a pulse train injected into a random subset of the neurons under the clamp, bounded in amplitude and duration by the level. Nothing is reset between shocks: the second one meets a network still adapted from the first.

What is measured. Population firing rates in 20 ms bins against the network's own quiet baseline, the onset of a response, bursts, how many regions were recruited, the undershoot after the train and the time back inside the baseline band. The trace above is that measurement. If a shock makes no distinguishable response, the card says so.

The flatline is a rule of the piece. The adaptation current of the network, averaged over the last 15 s, is a measured load. When it stays above 1.45 times its quiet level, which takes about ten level 5 shocks in a row and no amount of level 3, the background drive is cut and the spontaneous activity stops: the trace goes flat, the body hangs, he says nothing. A shock of level 3 or more restores the drive, the activity returns, and he talks about it. No neuron has such a rule; it is the piece's.

His lines. Reactions and comments written from the measured numbers, never a reading of a feeling, and after a strong response a plea: he asks whoever is pressing to stop, begs, bargains, turns to the others watching; the pleas escalate with his register. A character pleading in fiction. His screams are real ones: recordings of people screaming, re-voiced as him, because a speech engine cannot scream. A rule line is not said again while it is recent. His register (rattled, worn, wary; shown beside the phase) is read off the same load number, the shocks of the last minute and the time since a revival, and picks which lines are on offer; he also notices the same hand pressing again, a new hand, people watching without pressing, and someone arriving.

What is art. His body animates from three numbers read off the bins (rate above baseline, rate below it, adaptation above its baseline), through springs for the body's inertia; the camera, the lamp dip and his lines are a character's. The movements are artistic mappings of simulated signals, not human physiology. No living neurons and no hardware are connected. The character is fiction.
